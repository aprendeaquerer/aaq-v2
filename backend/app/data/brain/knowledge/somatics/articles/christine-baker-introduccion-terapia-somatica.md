For those of you who don't know me, I'm a licensed professional counselor with 13 years of experience working in trauma recovery. I'm also a somatic experiencing practitioner, and I strongly believe that mental health healing comes through addressing stuck places in the body. And that's exactly what this podcast is all about, offering you practical skills to build into a nervous system rhythm of care until you become someone who routinely thinks about nurturing your own nervous system. The most episodes of this podcast will be about 10 minutes or less focusing on a specific somatic practice to help support a specific mental health or relational need. These first 10 episodes are going to be different. So if this sounds like something you might be interested in, please take a moment to subscribe wherever you're listening or watching. Those couple of clicks can really help others find these resources too. So have you ever noticed how your body feels when you're stressed? Maybe your shoulders creep up towards your ears. Maybe your stomach ties itself in knots or your chest feels really tight. Or maybe you're the opposite, feeling really numb, disconnected, or maybe like you're floating outside of your body. These are not random sensations, they're your body's way of communicating with you. An understanding this language, this kind of communication, is what somatic therapy is all about. Today, we're diving into the basics of somatics, what it is, why it matters, and how it can transform your healing journey. At its core, somatic therapy is based on a fundamental truth that Dr. Peter Levine discovered through his groundbreaking work, that trauma and stress are not just in your head, they are stored in your body. When we experience overwhelming events, our nervous system can get stuck in these patterns of fight, flight, and freeze, even long after the danger has passed. Let me give you an example of how our bodies naturally process, stress, and overwhelm. So in nature, when animals face a threatening situation, their body automatically knows how to release that stress afterwards. They might shake or tremble or take deep breaths. These are all natural ways of the body returning to feeling safe again. But as humans, we often ignore or suppress these natural responses, we're kind of taught to get over it or push through. And this suppression can lead to that stress energy, that survival energy, getting stuck in our body, and that creates all kinds of physical and emotional symptoms. That is where somatic therapy comes in. The body-based approach that helps you recognize and track physical sensations, release stored tension and trauma, build a better relationship with your body, develop resources for self-regulation, and create new patterns of safety and connection. The beauty of somatic work is that it doesn't require you to relive traumatic experience or talk about them in detail as many other trauma therapies do. Instead, it focuses on the present moment, what you're feeling in your body right now and uses that awareness as a pathway to healing. As Dr. Bessel Vander Kolk explains, the body keeps the score of our life experiences, but it also holds the key to our healing. And so through simple somatic practice, we can begin to unlock this healing potential. In the coming episodes, we'll explore specific tools and techniques that you can use in your daily life. But for now, I want to leave you with a simple practice to try. Just for a moment, I want you to pause wherever you are. Take a gentle breath and just notice three sensations in your body. Maybe it's the weight of your feet on the ground, the temperature of the air on your skin, or the movement of your breath through your body. No need to change anything, just notice. That simple act of awareness is your first step in somatic practice.
